# Copy Patterns — Conversion Copywriting

**Primary owner:** scribe. All copy runs through the quality gate (`agency/skills-library/marketing/quality-gate.md`) before publishing. Brand voice lives in `agency/brands/<slug>/brand-voice.md` — read it first, always.

## Principles (in order)

1. **Clarity over cleverness.** If the reader has to decode your line, you've lost them. Clear copy converts: clearer positioning is associated with materially higher conversions, shorter sales cycles, and lower acquisition cost.
2. **Benefits over features.** Features: what it does. Benefits: what that means for the customer. Drill buyers want the hole, not the drill.
3. **Specificity over vagueness.** "Cut your weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" beats "save time on your workflow". Real numbers beat adjectives.
4. **Customer language over company language.** Mirror the words customers actually use — from reviews, comments, support tickets, DMs.
5. **One idea per section.** Each section advances one argument down the page.

## Style rules

- Simple over complex ("use" not "utilize"), specific over vague, active over passive, confident over qualified (cut "almost", "very", "really").
- Show over tell — describe the outcome instead of stacking adverbs.
- Honest over sensational. Fabricated stats or testimonials erode trust and create legal liability.
- No exclamation points. No marketing buzzwords without substance ("streamline", "innovative", "disruptive").
- Banned vocabulary (pattern-matches to AI slop / course-bro): "game-changing", "revolutionary", "10x", "secret", "hidden", "what they don't want you to know", "limited time" with no real time limit, inflated "$X value" claims with no comparable, "100% guaranteed" without conditions.

## Headline formulas

- "{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}"
- "The {category} for {audience}"
- "Never {unpleasant event} again"
- "{Question highlighting the main pain point}"
- Transformation arc (Human Action Model): current discomfort → better vision → path to action. Run every headline through the "Now you can ___" test — if the reader can't finish that sentence, the headline isn't specific enough.

## CTA formula

**[Action verb] + [What they get] + [Qualifier if needed]**

Weak: Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Click Here. Strong: "Get the Complete Checklist", "Start My Free Trial", "See Pricing for My Team".

## Page structure

Above the fold: headline → subheadline (1–2 sentences, adds specificity) → primary CTA. Then: social proof → problem/pain → solution/benefits (3–5) → how it works (3–4 steps) → objection handling (FAQ, guarantees) → final CTA with risk reversal.

## Psychology quick-reference

Apply ethically — persuasion, not manipulation. Real scarcity only; fake countdowns and invented stock levels destroy trust permanently.

| Problem | Levers to reach for |
|---|---|
| Low conversions | Reduce friction (Hick's Law: fewer choices), lower activation energy (trivial first step), B.J. Fogg: motivation × ability × prompt — all three must be present |
| Price objections | Anchoring (show higher price first), framing (90% success vs. 10% failure), mental accounting ("$1/day"), loss aversion (what they lose by not acting) |
| Building trust | Authority (credentials, featured-in), social proof (numbers, names), reciprocity (give value first), pratfall effect (admit a real weakness: "We're not the cheapest, but…") |
| Urgency | Real scarcity, loss aversion, Zeigarnik effect ("You're 80% done") |
| Retention | Endowment effect (free trials create ownership), status-quo bias (make switching away feel like loss), switching costs built ethically (integrations, saved data) |
| Decision paralysis | Paradox of choice: 3 tiers beat 7; pre-select the recommended option; nudge via defaults |

### High-leverage mental models

- **Theory of Constraints:** find the single bottleneck before optimizing elsewhere. Funnel converts but traffic is low? Fix traffic, not the funnel.
- **Local vs. global optima:** don't optimize the subject line if the channel itself is wrong. Zoom out before zooming in.
- **Inversion:** list everything that would guarantee this campaign fails, then systematically prevent each one.
- **Flywheel:** design content → traffic → customers → proof → more content cycles. Each element should power the next.
- **Rule of 7:** prospects need ~7 touchpoints. One post rarely converts; sustained presence compounds.
- **BJ Fogg:** behavior needs motivation, ability, AND a prompt — design all three into every conversion path.
