# Photo Sourcing and Credit

**Primary owner:** scout (find and verify), blueprint (use). Every cover and every in-story photo on a news site needs a known, credited source.

## Where to look, in order

1. **The site's own media library.** Search files created since the event. Descriptive filenames ("Gators take the field - Florida vs Ole Miss 2026-09-26.jpg") usually identify the photographer's upload.
2. **The staff or partner photographer's gallery** (listed in the brand's `editorial-desk.md`). New galleries often appear a day or more after an event. If today's isn't up, schedule a re-check instead of substituting something uncredited.
3. **Official team or athletic-department photos:** only where the brand's usage rights allow, credited exactly as the source asks.

## Provenance rules

- Match a photo someone sends you against the library by dimensions and content before assuming who took it.
- **Credit unknown means don't publish.** "It's either our photographer or the team's" is not a credit. Hold the photo and ask.
- Check resolution before using a photo as a hero: under about 1200px wide will look soft full-bleed.
- Put the credit in the alt text ("Photo by Chris Spears, GatorBait Media") and in the caption where the template shows one.
- Never use AI-generated illustration as the cover of a news story or column. Branded graphic covers (scoreboards, stat cards) are fine when they're labeled as the brand's own graphics.
- Never download and re-upload video from a rights holder; embed it.
- No cover reused across two live posts.
